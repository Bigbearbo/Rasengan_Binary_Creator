# Endpoint-only identification of a direct two-bit adder (companion prospective comparison)
For a student offered an integer affine, clipped, three-bit local result, measure how much final-answer supervision alone can identify its six local coefficients. Candidate coefficients and bias are each independently one of -2,-1,0,1,2: exactly 15,625 candidates. The desired zero-bias coefficient vector is in this hand-selected range. Sort by squared norm, then lexicographic order of (A0,A1,B0,B1,carry,bias); the first candidate consistent with every observed whole output is the learner. The learner sees no true carry, no partial labels, and no teacher wiring other than the authored affine structure, operand order, bit decoder, clipping, base-four partition and update semantics. One direct local cell emits a 0..3 digit and its carry from the clipped value; four autonomous recurrent steps emit the 9-bit sum. Starting carry zero.
Reuse the arithmetic cohort default_rng(20261120..20261135).choice(65536,64,replace=False), id=256*A+B. Report nested 1/2/4/8/16/32/64 paper budgets including candidate counts after selection, how many seeds admit exactly one coefficient vector, how many selected models have exact final sums and valid four-step traces over all 65,536 cases. Score each distinct selected local table exhaustively and cache. Repeat normative candidate filtering at identical budgets with the four true two-bit transition outputs per paper; count candidates and score selections with the same tie-break and search, thus controlling the hypothesis class. Use both data access costs, no model or hyperparameter selection. Equality of final scores does not imply equal supervision cost. The exhaustive search and small range offer an upper reference for information identifiability, not a claim about an ordinary gradient-trained network or discovery of the base-four decomposition. Archive checkpoints, seed sequences, candidate counts, selected truth tables, scoring logic, and hypotheses tested. No training outcomes observed before this protocol.
